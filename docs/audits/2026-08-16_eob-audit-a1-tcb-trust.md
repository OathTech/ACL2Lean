## Q1 answer — is the trust claim true as stated?

**Yes, for truth; qualified, for provenance.** A mirror's statement lives in a zero-import file; the kernel plus the classical trio is the entire trust base for its *truth* — a wrong `evalOpt`, a wrong square, a wrong emission, or a wrong transfer can make proofs fail or prove *waypoint/replayed* statements that mean something unintended, but cannot produce a kernel-accepted proof of a false core-Lean statement. The attack on the anchoring fails structurally: untrusted-layer constants appear only in proof terms, never in mirror statements (probed). What the kernel does NOT certify: (a) that the mirror Prop is a faithful rendering of the ACL2 book's theorem — that is the reader's reading of the spec file (e.g. `bsort` is rendered as length-many `bnext` passes, not the book's bad-pair-count recursion — flagged in the spec's own docstring); (b) that the proof arrived *via replay* — that is F1/F2's territory: generated templates + waypoint-layer gates + review, with two demonstrated (deliberate-construction-only) bypass routes.

"Every mirror theorem is a self-contained statement over its own zero-import definitions, kernel-checked from exactly propext/Classical.choice/Quot.sound — so nothing in the ACL2 pipeline, however wrong, can make one false; you need only read that one spec file and trust Lean's kernel. The further claim that these theorems were proved *via ACL2 replay* rather than by a Lean-side shortcut is not kernel-certified: it rests on generated proof templates, build-failing seam/axiom gates at the waypoint layer (whose per-book granularity and absence at the mirror level are known), and review — this audit verified it holds today for all nine live artifacts by direct proof-term inspection, and also demonstrated that a deliberate author could evade the template gate, so treat 'via replay' as strongly-evidenced engineering, not mathematics."
