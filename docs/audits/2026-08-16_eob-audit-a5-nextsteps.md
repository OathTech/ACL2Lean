### (b) The levers, with the structural facts that decide them

**Parallelism — already done; not a lever.** Coverage books import only `Tests.Coverage.Harness` (verified: `Tests/Coverage/BSsortsEquivalent.lean` header) and Lake schedules them concurrently. Measured: rebuilding BN11CustomMeasure (27s) + BSisort (52s) together took **61s wall at 186% CPU** — parallel already. The sweep's ~1.2h wall is the critical path: lib chain → Harness → **BSsortsEquivalent (34.3 min)** → aggregate. Shrinking sorts-equivalent IS shrinking the sweep.

**The main lever — the D7 transport, designed in 2026-07 and never built.** The transfer today (`crossBookRegistry`, `/home/dev/projects/ACL2Lean/ACL2Lean/Replay/Runner.lean:460`) re-replays each demanded dep theorem at each consumer's world, because a registry entry's statement is world-specific. But the world-monotonicity metatheorem this was supposed to use **already exists, proven, in the trusted core**: `evalOpt_world_mono` (`/home/dev/projects/ACL2Lean/ACL2Lean/EvalOpt.lean:712`, fuel-shape preserving, "so ∃N∀f≥N facts transfer directly") — built per `docs/plans/2026-07-10_external-knowledge-design.md` §D2, whose §D7 spells the intended assembly: replay once over `w_B`, transfer to `w_C ⊇ w_B` with the two decidable side conditions (`hext` = exactly today's `worldIncludes`; `hnew` = the no-shadow condition, dischargeable from the `proveNoShadow` facts the drivers already build). Grep confirms **no driver code consumes `evalOpt_world_mono` today**. Two stages:

1. **P-A1 — transport instead of re-replay, within the pre-pass.** For UNCONDITIONAL entries (at HEAD that is all 116 — the sprint's end state makes this lever newly available; hypotheses are contravariant so conditional entries cannot transport and keep today's route, fail-closed), replay the dep tree at the dep's OWN world and register the transported constant. Alone this doesn't reduce replay count (still once per consumer module) — its value is enabling stage 2 and hardening the semantics.
2. **P-A2 — share across consumers via the module DAG.** Each dep book's own module already `addDecl`s its replayed-statement constants during elaboration; if consumer coverage modules import their dep modules (corpus order = the existing `covDeps` DAG, `Tests/Coverage/Harness.lean:66-88`), the pre-pass becomes: look up the dep's own constant + `evalOpt_world_mono` transport + kernel-check — **each theorem and each admission replayed exactly once corpus-wide**. Estimated effect: SE's pre-pass ~19 min → seconds-to-a-minute of transports; sweep CPU 54 min → roughly 25–30; the critical path becomes the dep-chain of own-costs (perm→…→qsort→SE), of which qsort-own ≈ 7 min and SE-own + usefi ≈ 10–15 min. Claim gate plausibly 1.5h → ~50–60 min.
   Fail-closed audit: the transport is a kernel-checked lemma application; statements are still matched by statement; `worldIncludes` still refuses; invalidation inherits the existing `scripts/invalidate-coverage.sh` protocol unchanged (all book oleans die together, so no new staleness class); the golden's row text is untouched by construction (the `[cross-book …]` prints are stdout, not row lines). Golden must verify byte-identical — that is the acceptance test.

**Lazy / demanded-only pre-pass — mostly subsumed.** Post-P6 the seed is deliberately offer-widened (`bookDemandSeed`, Runner.lean:430 — 12 extra replays at SE, load-bearing for the last cond retirement). Narrowing it back is **disqualified** (it re-introduces the stranded-hypothesis class P6 closed). A two-pass "replay, collect kept conds, transfer only those" adds a second run of the consumer's own rows — a bad trade. Under P-A2 the widened seed's marginal cost is ~zero transports, and this whole question dissolves.

**usefi prepare pass (SE-specific follow-up question, not a claim):** the parametric equisort capstones already exist as library constants (`ACL2Lean/Imported/Waypoints/EquisortParametric.lean` — L3 world-parametric); whether `prepareUseFi` at the coverage layer re-derives per-sweep what those constants already carry is worth one scout before the perf arc scopes it in.

**Incremental dev-loop (cheap, zero gate risk):** the pieces exist — per-book sections in `Tests/coverage-actual/`, the focused CLI, `check-golden-current`. Two gaps: `just replay` does not pass `--deps`, so a consumer book cannot be focused-replayed at sweep parity without hand-typing 8 log paths (and even then usefi rows differ — my SE run shows ASSUMED rows where the sweep replays); and there is no "books affected by this diff" recipe. A `just replay-book &lt;name&gt;` that supplies the `covDeps` closure automatically is a ~30-line recipe. The full sweep stays the claim-gate truth — this is explicitly dev-loop only.

### (c) Ranking (win ÷ risk to fail-closed)

1. **P-A (transport + module-DAG sharing)** — biggest win (~20–30 min off every sweep, ~30–45 min off every claim gate, and the scaling story: today cost per added consumer book grows with its whole dependency cone, which is superlinear corpus growth — with 100+ books the sweep explodes without this). Risk LOW: the metatheorem is proven trusted-core content; behavior falls back to today's route wherever transport doesn't apply. ~1–2 lanes + 2–3 verification sweeps.
2. **Dev-loop recipe + `--deps` parity** — small absolute win, near-zero risk, improves every future lane's OODA loop. Hours.
3. **usefi-prepare scout** — unknown win (could be most of SE's remaining ~10 min), read-only scout first. Hours.
4. **Lazy demand** — subsumed; do not do separately.
5. **Parallelism** — nothing to do (already parallel; measured).

**Disqualified explicitly:** any cross-run persistent proof cache outside Lake's tracking (the sweep's IO-read artifacts are exactly where two stale-cache incidents already happened — P5a's catalog olean, the repin no-op; adding a cache layer there weakens the gate); narrowing the P6 demand seed; skipping full invalidation at claim points; weakening the golden compare to sections-only at claim points.
